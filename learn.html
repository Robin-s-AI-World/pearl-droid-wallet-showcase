01What Pearl is

Pearl is a Layer-1 blockchain — its own base network, like Bitcoin or Ethereum — secured by Proof-of-Useful-Work (PoUW). The native coin is PRL; its smallest unit is a grain (1 PRL = 100,000,000 grains).

The premise (from the paper arXiv 2504.09971): the thing GPUs are built for — multiplying big matrices — is also the dominant cost of running a neural network. So Pearl makes the proof-of-work be a real, verified matrix multiplication. The energy isn't wasted on an arbitrary hash; it produces a completed computation that has value on its own.

The moving parts

pearld — the full node. Validates blocks, relays transactions, and verifies the proofs. node/
Oyster — the HD wallet daemon. This is the engine inside the Pearl Wallet Android app. wallet/
vLLM miner — GPU mining built as a plug-in to vLLM, a real AI-inference server. miner/
pearl-gateway — the bridge between a miner and a node. miner/pearl-gateway/
zk-pow — the zero-knowledge circuit that proves the matmul was done correctly. zk-pow/
plonky2 · blake3 · XMSS — the proving system, the hash, and a post-quantum signature scheme. Rust / C

Heaviest-difficulty chain wins, Bitcoin-style — but a block is only valid if its useful-work proof verifies. Mainnet block time is 3 min 14 s (~π minutes), and difficulty re-tunes every block with a smooth weighted moving average (WTEMA), not Bitcoin's two-week jumps.

02The unit of work is a matrix multiply

A "matmul" is just rows-times-columns. To get one cell of the answer, you multiply a row of A by a column of B, pairwise, and add it up. Do that for every cell and you've multiplied the matrices. Press play and watch one result cell get built.

row · column → one cell

That's the entire physical "work" of a Pearl miner: a giant A × B. The GPU is a machine that does billions of these multiply-adds per second. In Bitcoin those cycles would be spent hashing nonsense; in Pearl they're spent on the same arithmetic an AI model needs for a forward pass — and the winning multiplication becomes the block.

03How a matmul becomes a block

Mining is still a lottery — but the lottery ticket is a real computation. The miner multiplies the matrices, folds the result into a hash, and checks whether that hash lands below the difficulty target. Most don't. The rare one that does is a winning block.

A × B (real matmul)→ fold + keyed Blake3→ hash→ hash ≤ target?
attempts: 0 · winners: 0

Three things stop a miner from cheating: the matmul is bound to this exact block (the inputs are derived from the block being mined, so you can't pre-compute once and reuse), the result must genuinely land below target, and — the clever part — the miner must attach a zero-knowledge proof that the multiplication was actually done correctly. Lie about the math and the proof fails at the node. That proof is section 5.

04Why you never see anyone's chats

The honest worry: "if my GPU is mining an LLM, am I processing people's private conversations?" No — and the architecture makes it impossible. A Pearl miner works at the linear-algebra layer, not the conversation layer. Watch what happens to a sentence on its way to your GPU.

a sentence enters the model…

By the time data reaches a linear layer deep inside a transformer, the text is long gone: it's been split into tokens, turned into embedding vectors, and pushed through many layers of math into an anonymous block of INT8 numbers. The Pearl plug-in slots in as a replacement for one of those multiply kernels. What it receives is literally two integer matrices, A and B — no tokens, no prompt, no user, no response, no logs.

The one-liner: a Pearl miner is the calculator that multiplies the numbers — it never reads the sentence those numbers came from, and couldn't, because the sentence isn't there anymore. Reconstructing a conversation from one layer's matrix would need the whole rest of the model, the tokenizer, and the original prompt — none of which a miner ever touches. vLLM (the host) runs the chat; the miner only intercepts the arithmetic.

05The proof: how the network trusts your math

A low hash isn't enough — anyone could claim one. The miner ships a certificate: a zero-knowledge proof that a specific, correct matrix multiplication produced that hash. The node verifies it in milliseconds without redoing the work. It's a three-layer recursive proof — tap each layer.

Layer 0 — the STARK · proves the multiplication really happened
A trace with three interleaved "chips" per row: a matmul chip that accumulates the inner products, a Blake3 chip that hashes the matrix slices into Merkle commitments, and a jackpot chip that XOR-folds the products into the value you hash against the target. It proves: the inputs are valid int7 numbers, they hash to the committed HASH_A/HASH_B, and the multiplication of those exact inputs equals the jackpot value. zk-pow/src/circuit/pearl_circuit.rs
Layer 1 — Plonky2 wrap · shrinks and re-verifies
A Plonky2 SNARK circuit that verifies Layer 0's STARK proof and checks its public inputs are internally consistent. This compresses a big STARK into a small, fast-to-check object.
Layer 2 — zero-knowledge wrap · hides everything but the verdict
A second Plonky2 circuit that verifies Layer 1 and turns on true zero-knowledge (salted Merkle leaves + blinding), so the proof reveals that the matmul was correct without revealing the matrices. The final certificate is a ~60 KB proof the node can reject or accept fast.

The proof's fingerprint — SHA256d(version ‖ public-data) — is written into the block header field ProofCommitment. That welds the proof to the header, and the header to the chain: you can't swap the proof after the fact without breaking the whole block. node/wire/blockheader.go certificate_v1.go / certificate_v2.go

06Solo, gateways, and pools

Generating that ZK proof is expensive. How you handle it is the difference between mining solo and mining in a pool. Toggle below.

On the wire: the only miner-facing protocol in the Pearl reference source is the gateway's small JSON-RPC — getMiningInfo (fetch work) and submitPlainProof (submit a candidate). The classic Bitcoin "Stratum" protocol (mining.subscribe/notify/submit) and specific pools like PearlHash or LuckyPool run their own external software on top of this model — their exact share/payout schemes live in their stacks, not in the open reference code, so treat pool-specific details as coming from each pool, not from the protocol itself.

07The MoE fork — v1 to v2

Pearl v2.0.0 ships a consensus hard fork. At a fixed block height the proof certificate switches from V1 dense to V2 MoE. Drag the slider through the fork height to see what flips.

height71,900

What "MoE" means here

Mixture-of-Experts is a model design where each token is routed to a few specialist sub-networks ("experts") instead of running every weight. Frontier models increasingly use it. The fork lets Pearl mine those models' matmuls — many per-expert multiplies with shared routing — not just single dense ones.

V1 certificate
Fixed 164-byte public data. One dense matmul. certificate_v1.go
V2 certificate
Variable public data (up to 4807 bytes) describing which experts ran. certificate_v2.go

Why a hard fork: at mainnet height 71935 the node requires a V2 certificate and rejects V1 blocks, and the proof's version prefix changes — so old and new nodes disagree about what's valid. The good news: the V2 prover is backward-compatible (it proves both old dense and new MoE work), so plain hashing/dense miners keep working — they just need the upgraded proving code before the fork, or their post-fork blocks bounce. This is exactly why this wallet re-vendored the v2 engine: a v1 wallet stops following the chain at 71935.
